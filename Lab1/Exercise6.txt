Design Report:
    
    There are 5 boys out of which 3 are telling the truth and two are lying. There is only one thief.
    Their statement need to be converted from natural language to predicates in order to compute with Haskell who is telling the truth, who is lying and who is the thief.

Approach:
    The idea is to compute each boys testimony while considering each boy one by one as the suspect.
    Once the testimonies are computed for each boy as the suspect, we compute the accusers for each boy usiong the accusers :: Boy -> [Boy] function and see for which boy as suspect 
    we get 3 accusers for the suspect. Since we know 3 boys are always telling the truth it means that if a suspect gets 3 accusers then that means
    than only he can be the thief. 
    Use the accuses :: Boy -> Boy -> Bool function to encode each boys' testimony under a variable suspect.
    For example Matthew's testimony "Matthew: Carl didn't do it, and neither did I" can be encoded as
    accuses Matthew suspect =
    suspect /= Carl && suspect /= Matthew
    ie Matthew accusses the suspect if the suspect is not Carl or Matthew, otherwise he does not accuse the suspect.

    Similarly, 
    Peter's testimony is "It was Matthew or it was Jack."
    accuses Peter suspect =
        suspect == Matthew || suspect == Jack
    And so on we can gather for each suspect who accuses them and who does not.

    In the accusers function we use the accuses function to check under the current suspect who accuses them and returning the list of accusers for that suspect.

    Once we find for which suspect the number of accusers is equal to 3 we know who the three honest boys and who the two dishonest boys were.
    And we know who was actually the thief.

Result:
    Guilty:
    Jack
    Honest:
    [Matthew,Peter,Carl]
